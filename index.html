The channel

A full-time job, a full house, and a camera on the lunch break.

I'm learning photography in public. Real shoots, honest assessments of my own work, and the misses shown alongside the keepers.

If good photographs can come out of the margins of a normal week, they can come out of yours too. That's the whole idea, and the channel is the evidence, one episode at a time.

  1. Wild Pockets

    Thirty-minute lunch sessions in a single park, three times a week, across a full year.

  2. Monthly report cards

    My work graded against a professional standard, with Claude as my coach: what fell short, and how I fixed it in the edit.

  3. Every setting shown

    Shutter, aperture, ISO and lens on every photograph, with the reasoning behind each choice. Disagree? Tell me.

  4. Short form

    Small subjects, early light and Florida skies, made in the pockets between everything else.

Flagship series

Wild Pockets

One park  ·  A year of lunches

For two years I walked the same stretch of sidewalk every day and fell for the small changes. Wild Pockets brings that habit back with a camera.

One park, wild pockets surrounded by mankind: herons working the water's edge, a squirrel with somebody's bread, a stranger on a dock, a man who hauled a keyboard out under an oak. Each season, taken for what it is. The series is becoming a book.

  • Three lunches a week
  • Thirty minutes each
  • One year

About

Zachery Seymour

I photograph wildlife and early light: herons at the water's edge, the first hour of sun, the small things most people walk past. I keep the surroundings in the frame, because the place is half the story.

I'm a Florida native, not a professional yet, and I'm learning in public: grading my own work honestly and showing the misses next to the keepers. Everything here was made with gear I already own and edited in Affinity and the free version of DaVinci Resolve.
